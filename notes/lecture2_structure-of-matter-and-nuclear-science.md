# PART I – THE STRUCTURE OF MATTER

## 1. Atoms and Nuclei
- All matter is made of atoms, which are made of **protons, neutrons and electrons**.
- "Atom" means *unsplittable*, which is untrue: particle accelerators (CERN, LHC) smash protons.

## 2. The Bohr Model of the Atom (Bohr–Rutherford, 1913)
- Negatively charged **electrons orbit** a central **nucleus** of protons and neutrons.
- **Planetary model:** electrons are the planets and the nucleus is the sun. The **electrostatic force** plays the role of gravity.
- **Nucleus:** positive **protons** plus neutral **neutrons**, collectively called **nucleons**.
- A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons, so its **net charge is zero**.
- **Atomic number Z** = number of protons. The periodic table is ordered by increasing Z.
- The **valence shell (quantum) model** is more accurate but more complex. Bohr's model is sufficient for this course.

### 2.1 Electron Arrangement
- Shells: n = 1 holds **2**, n = 2 holds **8**, n = 3 holds **18**. General rule: **2n²** electrons per shell.
- n = 1 is the **lowest-energy** shell, and energy increases with shell number.
- **Ground state:** electrons occupy the lowest energy levels, filling n = 1 first, then n = 2, and so on.
- **Valence electrons:** the outermost (highest-energy) electrons. They determine **bonding and reactivity**.

### 2.2 Properties of Subatomic Particles
| Particle | Charge (in units of e) | Mass (in units of mᵤ) |
|---|---|---|
| Electron | −1 | 1/1836 (≈ negligible) |
| Proton | +1 | 1 |
| Neutron | 0 | 1 |
- **e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C** (SI: A·s)
- **mᵤ = 1.661 × 10⁻²⁷ kg**

## 3. Nuclide Notation & Isotopes
- Written as ᴬ_Z X:
  - **Z** = atomic number (protons)
  - **A** = mass number (nucleons = protons + neutrons)
  - **N = A − Z** (neutrons)
- Examples: ¹₁H, ⁴₂He, ¹²₆C / ¹³₆C / ¹⁴₆C, ¹⁶₈O, ²⁴₁₂Mg, ²³⁵₉₂U, ³⁵₁₇Cl / ³⁷₁₇Cl
- **Isotopes:** same Z (same element), **different N** (so different A).
  - Carbon-12 (6n), carbon-13 (7n), carbon-14 (8n, unstable). 15 carbon isotopes are known.
  - There are just over **100 elements** and **over 2000 isotopes**, most of them artificial.

### 3.1 Ions
- Atoms are neutral. Many real-world elements exist as **ions**, with unequal protons and electrons.
- **Cations:** positive, because they have **lost electrons** (not gained protons).
- **Anions:** negative, because they have **gained electrons**.

| Ion | p | n | e |
|---|---|---|---|
| ²⁴₁₂Mg²⁺ | 12 | 12 | 10 |
| ²⁴₁₂Mg⁺ | 12 | 12 | 11 |
| ²⁴₁₂Mg⁻ | 12 | 12 | 13 |

## 4. The Periodic Table
- Ordered by **Z**. Horizontal rows are **periods**; vertical columns are **groups** (similar properties and valence electrons).
- **Most elements are metals.**
- **Group 1 – alkali metals:** 1 valence electron, so highly reactive, and more so further down the group. They form +1 cations. Soft (can be cut with a knife), shiny surface that tarnishes by oxidation, so they are **stored in oil**.
- **Group 2 – alkaline earth metals.**
- **Groups 3–12 – transition metals:** good conductors of heat and electricity, malleable, less reactive than alkali metals, high density and melting point (except mercury). Examples: Fe, Cu, Ag, Au.
- **Non-metals:** top right.
- **Metalloids:** on the step-like boundary between metals and non-metals, with properties of both. Si and Ge are **semiconductors** used in electronics.
- **Group 18 – noble (inert) gases:** full outer shells, so highly stable and unreactive. Odourless, colourless, monatomic. Example: **argon in light bulbs** stops the tungsten filament oxidising.
- **H and He** don't naturally fit any group (loosely placed in groups 1 and 18).

## 5. Mass Spectrometry
- An analytical technique that **identifies substances, even in trace amounts**, quickly and accurately.
- First designed in **1919 by Francis William Aston** to measure isotope masses (Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 1922).
- **Principle:** measure the masses and abundances of atoms/molecules by their **mass-to-charge ratio (m/z)**.

### 5.1 Stages (Time-of-Flight, ToF)
1. **Ionisation:** convert part of the sample into positive ions.
   - Electron ionisation (an electron beam knocks off electrons)
   - Chemical ionisation (chemical reactions)
   - Electrospray ionisation (high voltage applied to an aerosol sprayed through a nozzle)
2. **Acceleration / mass analysis:**
   - **ToF:** an electric field accelerates the ions. **Lighter ions travel faster** (same charge, usually +1).
   - **Magnetic sector:** a magnetic field bends the ions along curved paths that depend on charge-to-mass ratio.
3. **Detection:**
   - ToF: the ions trigger a current and the flight time is measured.
   - Magnetic sector: the ions strike different detector regions.
   - The strength of the current indicates **abundance**.

### 5.2 Mass Spectrograph (Spectrum)
- **Peak height** = abundance. **x-axis position** = mass-to-charge ratio.
- Each substance has a **unique signature**. Examples shown: cocaine and TNT.

### 5.3 Security Applications
- Drug detection, explosive detection, blood-alcohol measurement, identifying radioactive materials, forensic trace evidence.

# PART II – NUCLEAR SCIENCE

## 6. Radioactivity
- **Radioactive decay:** "the spontaneous disintegration of the nuclei of some nuclides" (**radionuclides**). Unstable nuclei emit radiation to become stable.
- Emissions: **α**, **β⁻**, **β⁺**, possibly accompanied by **γ**.
- α, β⁻ and β⁺ **change Z**, creating a **new element**.
- **Background radiation:** radon gas in air, food and drink, cosmic rays.

## 7. Types of Radiation

### 7.1 Alpha (α)
- **2 protons + 2 neutrons**, identical to a **helium nucleus** (⁴₂He²⁺).
- Charge **+2e**, mass **4 mᵤ**, speed ≈ 3 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹.
- Effect: **Z − 2, A − 4**.

### 7.2 Beta-minus (β⁻)
- An **electron emitted from the nucleus**, **not** from the electron shells. It comes from energy changes during disintegration.
- Charge −e, mass 1/1836 mᵤ, speed ≈ 2.9 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. Written ⁰₋₁e.
- A **neutron changes into a proton** and emits an electron, so **Z + 1, N − 1** (A unchanged). New element.

### 7.3 Beta-plus (β⁺, positron)
- A "positively charged electron" (positron / anti-electron). This is particle physics, beyond the course focus.
- Charge +e, mass 1/1836 mᵤ.
- A **proton changes into a neutron** and emits a positron, so **Z − 1, N + 1** (A unchanged). New element.

### 7.4 Gamma (γ)
- An **EM wave** emitted from the nucleus after an energy change.
- **No charge, no mass.** Does not change the numbers of protons or neutrons.
- Highest frequency and shortest wavelength in the EM spectrum: **10⁻¹⁰ to 2 × 10⁻¹³ m**. Speed c.

### 7.5 Summary Table
| Name | Symbol | Composition | Mass | Charge | Speed (m s⁻¹) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha | α / ⁴₂He | 2p + 2n | 4 mᵤ | +2e | ~3 × 10⁷ |
| Beta | β⁻ | electron | 1/1836 mᵤ | −e | ~2.9 × 10⁸ |
| Positron | β⁺ | anti-electron | 1/1836 mᵤ | +e | ~2.9 × 10⁸ |
| Gamma | γ | EM wave | 0 | 0 | ~3 × 10⁸ (c) |

### 7.6 Penetration & Ionisation
| Radiation | Range in air | Stopped by | Ionising power |
|---|---|---|---|
| α | a few cm | paper / outer skin | **Strongest** (very dangerous if **ingested**) |
| β± | a few m | wood, plastic, thin metal (penetrates a few cm of skin) | Medium |
| γ | large distances | high-Z materials, e.g. **lead** | Weakest |
- All three are **ionising radiation**: they eject electrons from atoms and molecules. This causes tissue damage, radiation sickness, cancer, mutation and death.
- X-rays are **non-nuclear** ionising radiation (Lecture 4).

## 8. Stability of Nuclei
- Protons repel each other. The **strong force** (a short-range attraction between nucleons) holds the nucleus together.
- **Binding energy** (associated with the strong force) determines stability.
- **N vs Z plots:**
  - Smallest stable nucleus: ¹₁H. Largest stable: **²⁰⁹₈₃Bi** (bismuth).
  - **Rule (i): N ≈ Z for Z < 20.**
  - **Rule (ii): N > Z for Z > 20.** Extra neutrons separate the protons and reduce repulsion.
- **Neutron-rich** (above the line of stability) decay by **β⁻** (n → p).
- **Proton-rich** (below the line) decay by **β⁺** (lighter nuclides) or **α** (heavier nuclides) (p → n).

## 9. Activity and Half-Life
- **Henri Becquerel** discovered radioactivity in uranium and shared the **1903 Nobel Prize** with Marie and Pierre Curie.
  - Uranium appeared to emit endlessly, but it has been decaying slowly since Earth formed about 4500 million years ago.
- **Becquerel (Bq):** the unit of **activity**. **1 Bq = 1 disintegration per second.** SI: **s⁻¹**.
- **1 curie (Ci) = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq.**
- **Half-life (t½):** "the time in which the amount of a radionuclide decays to half its original value."

| Nuclide | t½ |
|---|---|
| Uranium-238 | ~4.5 billion years |
| Uranium-235 | 25,000 years (as stated in the slides) |
| Carbon-14 | 5,730 years (carbon dating) |
| Hydrogen-3 (tritium) | 12.32 years |
| Hydrogen-7 | 21 × 10⁻²⁴ s (21 yoctoseconds) |
| Polonium-210 | 138 days |
- All decay follows a characteristic **exponential decay curve**: N = N₀ (½)^(t/t½).

## 10. Decay Equations & Decay Chains
- Use nuclide notation. **Mass numbers (A) and atomic numbers (Z) must balance** on both sides.
- **α decay (Pu-239, used in nuclear warheads):** ²³⁹₉₄Pu → ²³⁵₉₂U + ⁴₂He + energy
- **β⁻ decay (C-14, radiocarbon dating):** ¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₇N + ⁰₋₁e + energy
- **Parent** nuclide → **daughter** nuclide.
- **Decay chain (radioactive series):** a daughter may itself be unstable and decay further, until a stable nuclide is reached.
  - Shown on an **A vs Z plot**. Example: **Thorium-228 → Lead-208**.
  - α: A drops by 4, Z drops by 2. β⁻: Z rises by 1, A unchanged.

# CASE STUDY – A "Mini Nuclear Attack" in London (Litvinenko)

## 11. Background
- **Alexander Litvinenko:** ex-officer of the Russian **FSB** (formerly the KGB), specialising in organised crime.
- Fled to the UK in 2000 and was granted asylum. Became a journalist and worked for British intelligence. A critic of Putin; wrote *Blowing Up Russia* and *Lubyanka Criminal Group*.
- Described as "state-sponsored nuclear terrorism" and "a mini nuclear attack on the streets of London" (Chairman Sir Robert Owen).

## 12. The Assassination
- **1 November 2006:** drank tea laced with **Polonium-210** at the **Millennium Hotel, Mayfair**.
- Met **Andrey Lugovoy and Dmitry Kovtun**, the prime suspects, who are protected by Russia from extradition.
- Hospitalised 3 days later; slow, painful death over **23 days**.

## 13. Polonium-210
- Discovered by Marie and Pierre Curie in 1897. One of 25 radioactive isotopes of Po.
- A silver-coloured rare metal found in uranium ores (100 µg per tonne of ore).
- **Alpha emitter**, decays to **lead**. **Half-life 138 days** (intensely radioactive).
- Only about **100 g manufactured per year**, in nuclear reactors. The amount used would cost **tens of millions of dollars**. This points to a **state-sponsored** attack.
- **Alpha particles are contained by glass**, so it can easily be smuggled through airports.
- Maximum safe dose: **7 picograms (7 × 10⁻¹² g)**. An ingested speck is fatal.
- **Biological half-life about 30 days.** It steals electrons, causing DNA damage, genetic changes, cell destruction and organ failure.
- A sub-microscopic amount was enough to endanger thousands of people.

## 14. Detection & Identification
- **Geiger counter:** measures ionising radiation using ionising "events" in a **Geiger–Müller tube**. Reads out in counts per second and can give audible clicks.
- **Alpha-particle spectrometer:** identifies the source (similar to a mass spectrometer). Po-210 alphas have a characteristic energy of **5.3 MeV**.
- **Forensic trail:** radiation traces (hotel tea room, the teapot) left across London "like the breadcrumbs left by Hansel and Gretel".

## 15. Aftermath & Inquest
- Polonium poisoning was confirmed on **23 November 2006**, and he died hours later.
- The post-mortem was among the most dangerous ever undertaken: double protective suits and a filtered-air hood. Buried in a **lead-lined coffin** (Highgate Cemetery).
- The inquiry (began 2013, report **21 January 2016**) concluded that Lugovoy and Kovtun were "probably" acting for the FSB, "probably" approved by Putin.
- Actions taken: assets frozen, extradition sought (refused by Russia), Russian ambassador summoned.

## Relevant Tools & Technologies
| Tool | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Time-of-Flight mass spectrometer** | Separates ions by m/z using electric-field acceleration and flight time |
| **Magnetic sector mass spectrometer** | Separates ions by curved paths in a magnetic field |
| **Mass spectrograph** | Output: peak height = abundance, x-position = m/z (drug/explosive signatures) |
| **Geiger counter (Geiger–Müller tube)** | Detects and counts ionising radiation events |
| **Alpha-particle spectrometer** | Identifies alpha-emitting isotopes by characteristic energy (Po-210: 5.3 MeV) |
| **Particle accelerator (CERN/LHC)** | Shows atoms are splittable |
| **Lead shielding** | Stops γ-rays |
| **Carbon dating** | Uses C-14 β⁻ decay (t½ = 5,730 yr) |
