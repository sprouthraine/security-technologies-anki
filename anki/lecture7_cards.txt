Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Define RFID	<b>Radio Frequency Identification</b>: a technology that uses radio-frequency <b>electromagnetic fields</b> to <b>identify and track transponders (tags)</b> containing electronically stored information; tags attach to objects, animals or people. A <b>two-way communication</b> system (reader ↔ tag).	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	RFID applications in the urban landscape	<ul><li><b>Access control &amp; ID:</b> biometric passports/borders, secure workplaces, gyms/lockers, car/bike sharing (Zipcar)</li><li><b>Transport flow:</b> NY <b>E-ZPass</b></li><li><b>Payment:</b> <b>Oyster card</b>, contactless (Android Pay)</li><li><b>Loss prevention:</b> retail item tagging</li><li><b>Goods tracking</b>, including animals</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Why was RFID developed, and its advantages over barcodes	Originally to <b>replace barcodes in supply chains</b>: <b>wireless</b>, much <b>further distances</b>, <b>more information</b>, <b>more robust</b>, <b>simultaneous reading</b> of many tags (pallets).	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Two key components of an RFID tag	<ul><li><b>Microchip (IC):</b> a few components, e.g. <b>capacitor, diode, transistor</b></li><li><b>Antenna coil</b> (can be printed)</li></ul>Some tags are only a few mm; active tags also contain a <b>battery</b>.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	What are passive RFID tags?	The <b>most common</b> type (related to NFC); penny to credit-card size; <b>no internal power source</b>: they get <b>all their power by electromagnetic induction</b> from the reader's field.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Drivers of global RFID growth	Falling production costs (cheap ICs, <b>RFID printing</b>); tags built into goods at manufacture; retailers' stock tracking and loss prevention (50% of the top 100 in 2016); smart cards (travel, payment); need for <b>secure access control</b> (city growth, terror threat). Est. <b>USD 40.5 bn by 2025</b>.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	RFID attack: replay	<b>Intercept and record</b> the modulated backscatter signal (e.g. as a person enters a building), then <b>replay the same code to the reader later</b>, causing a security breach.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	RFID attacks: unauthorised scanning and reprogramming	<ul><li><b>Unauthorised scanning:</b> spy on a person (ID, medication revealing illness, passport biometrics)</li><li><b>Reprogramming:</b> make an expensive item read as cheap</li></ul>Both require the <b>encryption system to be known or hacked</b>.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	RFID attacks: denial of service and kill command	<ul><li><b>DoS / jamming:</b> RFID frequencies are well known, so an <b>interfering signal</b> makes reader–tag communication impossible</li><li><b>Kill command abuse:</b> some tags have a kill command; an adversary could disable a card with important <b>medical or financial</b> information</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Explain step by step how a passive RFID tag generates a modulated backscatter signal	<ol><li>Reader sends an RF signal through its (loop) antenna</li><li>The EM field <b>induces a voltage</b> in the tag antenna, giving a small current</li><li>The current <b>charges a capacitor</b>, which <b>powers the IC</b></li><li>The IC outputs its <b>unique ID</b> as <b>1s (high V) and 0s (low V)</b></li><li>The RF signal is sent back to the reader, but first modified by a <b>transistor</b></li><li>The transistor <b>switches the signal on/off</b> per the IC's voltages, embedding the ID: the <b>modulated backscatter signal</b></li><li>The reader <b>decodes</b> it and identifies the tag (or not)</li></ol>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	What is Ampère's law (as used in RFID read-range theory)?	A current flowing through a conductor produces a <b>magnetic field</b> around the conductor. A circular loop antenna with N turns produces a field at a point on its axis.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Key observation from the loop-antenna field equation for RFID	<b>B ∝ 1/r³</b>: the field decays very rapidly with distance. This is the <b>main limiting factor on RFID read range</b>; the equation is used to calculate reader range/sensitivity.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Three ways to increase the read range of an RFID reader (from B = µ₀INa²/2r³)	Increase the <b>current I</b>, the <b>number of turns N</b>, or the <b>loop radius a</b> (B ∝ a²).	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Define the weber and the henry	<ul><li><b>Weber (Wb):</b> unit of <b>magnetic flux</b> (density of field lines in an area); a change of 1 Wb/s induces 1 V EMF (Faraday). SI: kg m² s⁻² A⁻¹</li><li><b>Henry (H):</b> unit of <b>inductance</b>. SI: kg m² s⁻² A⁻²</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Pros of passive RFID tags	<ul><li><b>Cheap:</b> under 1p per tag, no batteries (but reader infrastructure costs)</li><li><b>Simultaneous reading:</b> a few hundred at a time</li><li><b>Long shelf life:</b> replace only if the coil/IC is damaged</li><li><b>Variable ranges:</b> LF 10 cm, HF &lt; 3 m, UHF up to 100 m</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Semi-passive vs active RFID tags	<ul><li><b>Semi-passive:</b> battery powers the IC/circuitry, so <b>quicker response</b>; still backscatter</li><li><b>Active:</b> battery powers the IC <b>and a transmitter</b>; return signal is <b>broadcast</b> (not backscatter), a bespoke signal with more info and <b>longer range</b>; <b>expensive</b>; used for high-value goods over long distances (cars/parts on production lines)</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	What is a systematic review?	A literature review that collects and <b>critically analyses multiple studies</b> to find what can reliably be said.<br><ul><li>Clear, <b>reproducible</b> strategy: specified databases, <b>pre-defined search terms</b></li><li>Clear <b>inclusion/exclusion rules</b></li><li>Explicit, systematic methods, so <b>less bias</b> than standard reviews</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	What is EMMIE and who developed it?	An evidence rating / <b>coding system</b> designed for <b>crime prevention</b> systematic reviews, distilling their quality and coverage across 5 dimensions (<b>Effect, Mechanisms, Moderators, Implementation, Economic costs</b>). Johnson, Tilley &amp; Bowers (2015).	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Apply each EMMIE dimension to RFID tagging in retail (the question asked)	<ul><li><b>Effect:</b> are tags effective at reducing theft?</li><li><b>Mechanisms:</b> through what mechanisms do they reduce theft?</li><li><b>Moderators:</b> what conditions moderate effectiveness?</li><li><b>Implementation:</b> what are the implementation considerations?</li><li><b>Economics:</b> what are the costs and returns?</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Retail crime context and loss prevention spending	Global cost over <b>$214 bn</b> (2014–15); leads to business closure and higher prices. ~<b>0.65% of sales</b> spent on loss prevention (store detectives, CCTV, locked displays, packaging, staff screening, signage, alarms, floor design, RFID tags).	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	What are EAS tags and their main advantage?	<b>Electronic Article Surveillance</b> tags (incl. RFID), used for apparel, groceries, books, electronics. Advantage: tagged products <b>remain on open display</b>, accessible to customers and staff.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Sidebottom et al. inclusion criteria	<ul><li>Explicit goal of reducing <b>theft, shrinkage or loss</b> via security tags</li><li>Specifically <b>retail</b> environments</li><li>At least <b>one quantitative outcome</b> measure</li><li><b>Original research</b> findings</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	EMMIE Effect: findings of the retail tagging review	<b>8 studies</b>, outcome usually <b>shrinkage</b>: <b>5 positive, 2 negative (backfire), 1 no significant change</b>, so mixed. Factors: hard vs soft tags, staff training/awareness, ease of removal, colour.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	What is the backfire effect in retail tagging?	Faulty tags, tags not disabled correctly, and system issues cause <b>high false-alarm rates</b>, so <b>staff are less likely to react</b> to alarms and tagging can increase theft.	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	EMMIE Mechanisms: how do tags reduce theft?	<ul><li><b>Increased risk:</b> <b>perceived</b> (offender sees tags and chooses not to steal) and <b>actual</b> (alarm, staff detain offender)</li><li><b>Reduced reward:</b> item <b>damaged</b> if the tag is removed illegitimately (ink tags, apparel)</li><li><b>Increased effort:</b> harder to remove tagged items; tag removability becomes an <b>arms race</b></li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	EMMIE Moderators of tag effectiveness (5)	<ol><li><b>Shop setting and staff attitude</b></li><li><b>Customers</b> (including thieves)</li><li><b>Tag type</b></li><li><b>Product</b></li><li><b>Police and criminal justice system</b></li></ol>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Moderator: shop setting and staff attitude	<ul><li>Unalarmed doors are attractive low-risk exits; some areas allow tag removal; <b>signage</b> reminds shoppers</li><li>Effectiveness <b>relies heavily on staff response</b></li><li>False alarms reduce staff confidence and anger shoppers, so staff wave people through (−); but they remind thieves tags are in use (+)</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Casual vs professional shoplifters: which tagging strategy works best?	<ul><li><b>Casual</b> (opportunistic, amateur, impulsive, unplanned): <b>high-profile tags + significant publicity</b></li><li><b>Professional</b> (plan ahead, steal to order for resale, analyse store weaknesses, know which tags can be circumvented): <b>short-term and covert tags</b>; thieves aren't fully aware of the risk, which leads to arrests</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Hard vs soft tags; selective tagging; display	<ul><li><b>Hard:</b> conspicuous (overt), conveys risk of apprehension</li><li><b>Soft:</b> inconspicuous (covert); relies on staff vigilance; spotted only when the alarm sounds</li><li><b>Selective/fractional:</b> tag high-value / frequently stolen goods or a proportion</li><li><b>Display:</b> show only part of an item (one shoe/glove)</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	Moderator: police and criminal justice system	<ul><li>Effectiveness depends on <b>arrest, conviction, punishment</b></li><li><b>Slow arrest:</b> staff deal with violent offenders, are off the floor, no guarantee of conviction</li><li><b>Removing a tag is not illegal; walking out without paying is</b>, so one offender de-tags and passes the item on</li><li>Punishment may be 'worth the risk'</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	EMMIE Implementation: source vs in-store tagging	<ul><li><b>Source:</b> proper, consistent application; saves staff time; but manufacturers use <b>different tag types/systems</b> (need multiple systems or supplier unification)</li><li><b>In-store:</b> correct attachment needed (too easy / too hard to remove)</li><li>Correct <b>deactivation</b> at point of sale and avoiding <b>double-tagging</b> (both cause false alarms)</li><li>One system for all merchandise</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	EMMIE Economics of tagging	<ul><li><b>Costs:</b> tags (type, reusability); infrastructure (gates, removal/deactivation equipment); employees (gate security, training, tagging/de-tagging time)</li><li><b>Returns:</b> reduced <b>shrinkage</b>; efficient <b>stock-checking</b> tool</li></ul>	SecTech L7 concept

N = 2Br³/(µ₀Ia²) = (2 × 10⁻⁶ × 0.1³) / (4π×10⁻⁷ × 0.02 × 0.04²)<br>= 2×10⁻⁹ / 4.021×10⁻¹¹ = 49.7, so <b>50 turns</b> (round up)<br>(Exact formula gives 63.)	SecTech L7 calculation

Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[4 marks] (Problem sheet Q2) Describe a method by which the security of an RFID system/tag may be compromised.	<b>Model answer (replay attack):</b><br><ul><li>An attacker uses a receiver to <b>intercept and record</b> the modulated backscatter signal transmitted by a legitimate tag (e.g. as an employee enters a building)</li><li>The recorded identifier is stored</li><li>Later the attacker <b>replays the same code</b> to the reader</li><li>The reader accepts it as genuine, giving <b>unauthorised access</b></li></ul><b>Alternatives:</b> unauthorised scanning/spying; reprogramming (cheap price); DoS jamming on known frequencies; kill command disabling medical/financial cards	SecTech L7 exam-style
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[5 marks] (Problem sheet Q3) Explain how a modulated backscatter signal is generated in a passive RFID tag through electromagnetic induction.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li>Reader transmits an RF signal via a <b>loop antenna</b></li><li>The EM field <b>induces a voltage/current</b> in the tag's antenna coil (Faraday's law), with <b>no battery</b></li><li>The current <b>charges a capacitor</b>, which <b>powers the IC</b></li><li>The IC outputs its <b>unique ID</b> as high/low voltages (1s and 0s)</li><li>A <b>transistor switches</b> the returning RF signal on/off according to the ID, giving the <b>modulated backscatter</b></li><li>The reader decodes it and identifies the tag</li></ul>	SecTech L7 exam-style
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[5 marks] (Problem sheet Q4) A new HF RFID reader loop antenna has radius 4 cm and current 20 mA. (B_min = 1×10⁻⁶ Wb m⁻²)	<ul><li>State B = µ₀INa²/(2r³) (a² ≪ r²) and rearrange: <b>N = 2Br³/(µ₀Ia²)</b></li><li>Convert: a = 0.04 m, r = 0.1 m, I = 0.02 A</li><li>N = (2 × 1×10⁻⁶ × 1×10⁻³)/(4π×10⁻⁷ × 0.02 × 1.6×10⁻³) = 2×10⁻⁹/4.021×10⁻¹¹</li><li>N = 49.7, so <b>50 turns</b> (round up to meet the minimum)</li></ul>	SecTech L7 exam-style

Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[6 marks] Compare passive, semi-passive and active RFID tags and state the frequency bands and read ranges of RFID systems.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li><b>Passive:</b> no battery, powered by induction, modulated backscatter; cheap (&lt; 1p); long shelf life; simultaneous reads; most common</li><li><b>Semi-passive:</b> battery powers the IC, giving faster response</li><li><b>Active:</b> battery powers the IC and a <b>transmitter</b>; broadcasts a bespoke signal; more info, longest range; expensive; high-value goods tracking</li><li><b>LF 125–134 kHz (~10 cm); HF 13.56 MHz (&lt; 3 m); UHF 433 / 865–960 MHz (up to 100 m)</b></li></ul>	SecTech L7 exam-style
Basic	Security Technologies::L7 RFID & EMMIE	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[5 marks] Explain the factors that determine the read range of a passive RFID system, with reference to the magnetic field of a loop antenna.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li>Ampère's law: current in the loop produces a magnetic field</li><li><b>B = µ₀INa²/[2(a²+r²)^(3/2)] ≈ µ₀INa²/(2r³)</b> when a ≪ r</li><li><b>B ∝ 1/r³</b>: rapid decay is the main limit on read range</li><li>Increase I, N or a to extend range (B ∝ I, N, a²)</li><li>The tag needs a minimum field to induce enough current to power the IC; frequency band also sets typical range (LF/HF/UHF)</li></ul>	SecTech L7 exam-style
