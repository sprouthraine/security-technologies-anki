Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Why is equating 'opportunity' with the situation incomplete (Ekblom)?	Opportunity is an <b>ecological interaction between situation and offender</b>. An open window three floors up is only an opportunity to someone with the <b>resources</b> (agility, courage, a ladder). And opportunity requires a <b>goal</b>: opportunity to do *what*?	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Elements needed for a full characterisation of opportunity	<ul><li><b>Perception/affordance</b>: can the offender see and understand it?</li><li><b>Goals</b> (positive + hygiene)</li><li><b>Resources/capability</b></li><li><b>Presence/access</b> (incl. telepresence via internet, drones)</li><li><b>Actions/scripts</b></li><li><b>Dynamics</b>: encountering, seeking, creating opportunities</li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Positive goals vs hygiene goals	<b>Positive:</b> what the offender wants to achieve (steal the car / get the money). <b>Hygiene:</b> what they want to avoid while doing it (arrest, injury).	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	How does Rational Choice relate to opportunity and risk (ISO 31000)?	Rational Choice factors <b>risk of harm, effort, reward</b> all relate to goal states and all contain a risk element; ISO 31000 defines <b>risk = effect of uncertainty on objectives</b>.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Definitions of 'problem' and 'problem solving' used in the lecture	<ul><li><b>Problem:</b> 'the difference between a goal state and a current state' (<b>Jonassen 2000</b>)</li><li><b>Problem solving:</b> 'the generation and selection of discretionary actions to bring about a goal state' (<b>Scandura 1977</b>)</li><li>Ekblom: what stops exploiting an opportunity being 'child's play'; purpose that is frustrated</li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	How are opportunity and problem related? Why is this 'flip' useful?	One party's opportunity is another's (or the state's) <b>problem</b>. Useful for <b>conflicts</b> (entangled problems/opportunities) and <b>arms races</b> (each move sets a problem for the opponent, who finds an 'adjacent opportunity' as a countermove).	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Definition of technology used by Ekblom (adapted from W. Brian Arthur)	Principles derived from <b>natural phenomena</b>, realised as applications and products in more or less complex combinations, to meet some <b>criminal or security goal</b>.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Four roles technology can play in crime/prevention (with examples)	<ul><li>Part of the <b>opportunity or problem</b> (CCTV can monitor misbehaviour OR spy on changing rooms; car alarms)</li><li>Helps either side <b>adapt to conditions</b> (street lighting)</li><li><b>Halts</b> a launched attack (personal attack alarm)</li><li><b>Mitigates</b> consequences (backing up phone data)</li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Three ways to reduce the risk of a crime type	<ol><li><b>Eliminate</b> the possibility (designing out car tax discs removes forgery)</li><li><b>Reduce the likelihood</b></li><li><b>Reduce the harm</b>, during (injury in robbery) or after (ID theft from a stolen wallet)</li></ol>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Strategic preventive actions from the speaker notes (anticipate, learning path…)	<ul><li><b>Anticipate:</b> crime impact statements (could it be a target, tool, or environment for crime?)</li><li><b>Learning path:</b> feed info on new crimes back to designers, planners, insurers</li><li><b>Encourage variety</b> (criminals love standardisation)</li><li><b>Performance standards</b>, research-based</li><li><b>Exploit new tech</b> for prevention</li><li><b>Avoid rigidity</b> (remember the dinosaurs)</li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Civil-world trade-offs that constrain security technology	Security vs: <b>convenience, social inclusivity, market freedom, sustainability, freedom of movement, aesthetics, health &amp; safety, privacy, trust &amp; collective efficacy</b>. Offenders are less constrained.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	What is a crime script and a script clash?	<b>Crime script</b> (Derek Cornish): the procedure for preparing for and committing a crime. Both offenders and preventers use scripts, giving <b>tactical script clashes</b> between them.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	List the generic script clashes (offender v preventer)	<ul><li>Wield force v resist</li><li>Conceal intent v detect</li><li>Conceal traces v detect them</li><li>Challenge suspect v give plausible response</li><li>Snoop v maintain privacy</li><li>Act at will v control misbehaviour</li><li>Take v keep target asset</li><li>Confront v avoid</li><li>Surprise/ambush v be alert</li><li>Trap v elude</li><li>Surveil v conceal</li><li>Pursue v escape</li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Why do script clashes matter for technology design?	They are <b>generic and perennial</b>; technology can <b>disrupt the balance</b> and favour one side after a breakthrough, so we must <b>design to advantage the good side</b>.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Desirable techno-trends for tweaking trade-offs and biasing clashes	<ul><li><b>Creative leap, not compromise</b> (internal combustion engine gave armour AND mobility)</li><li><b>Tunability</b> to context ('what works' is context-dependent)</li><li><b>Smart discriminator functions</b> (serve users, thwart thieves)</li><li><b>Adaptable/reconfigurable form</b> (swing-down fire escapes)</li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	What is the 'smart discriminator' problem?	What's good for legitimate users (smaller, lighter, portable, durable, cheap, easy) is also good for thieves. How do you serve one while thwarting the other? <b>User-friendly but abuser-unfriendly</b>.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Examples of large-scale evolutionary change creating crime opportunities	<ul><li><b>Godfrey-Smith:</b> cultural-phylogenetic change, e.g. the <b>Neolithic Revolution</b> (farming, density, stealable property)</li><li><b>Felson &amp; Eckert:</b> <b>mass transportation</b> changing routine activities</li><li>Now: <b>ICT, generative/agentic AI, LLMs</b></li></ul>Script clashes can flip to favour offenders, causing obsolescence.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Examples of offender–preventer co-evolution (arms races)	Banknote security, tax evasion, <b>car theft (Rick Brown)</b>, malware, script kiddies, rogue AI; classic case: <b>the safe (Neil Shover)</b>.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	How has the spread of criminal know-how accelerated?	From <b>pubs and prisons</b>, to the <b>internet</b>, guidance documents, video tips, to <b>crime-as-a-service</b> (e.g. Ransomware-as-a-Service) and <b>AI agents</b>.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Gearing up against crime: the dynamic strategy for arms races	<ul><li>Encourage <b>variety</b></li><li>Build capacity to <b>out-innovate</b> offenders</li><li><b>Performance standards</b> / generic principles</li><li><b>Study offender resources</b></li><li><b>Exploit new technology</b></li><li><b>Avoid rigidity &amp; lock-in</b></li><li><b>Future-proofing</b>; <b>pipelines of innovation</b></li><li><b>Prepare for disruption</b> (quantum crypto/cracking)</li><li><b>Learn from other evolutionary struggles</b></li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Other evolutionary struggles to learn from, and one lesson	<b>Military/hybrid warfare, predator–prey, pest–farmer, bacteria–antibiotic, immune system–virus.</b> E.g. gazelles use <b>remote sensing</b> (vision evolved 60+ times) and integrate senses to avoid <b>false alarms</b>; antibiotics show 'wonder solutions' are impermanent; IBM's artificial immune systems.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Hard vs soft approaches: the Victoria Centre example	Youths spitting and dropping cans in the Victoria Centre, Nottingham, were moved on <b>softly</b> with an uncomfortable rail, a sloped floor and hot lights, so they left <b>without realising they were manipulated</b> and weren't motivated to retaliate. Hard isn't always best (the reed and the oak).	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	What is TRIZ and how does it relate to crime prevention?	Theory of inventive problem solving from analysis of patents:<br><ul><li><b>40 inventive principles</b>; <b>39 contradiction parameters</b>; lookup tables</li><li><b>Evolutionary trends</b> (solid → segmented → flexible → field)</li><li>Addresses the fundamental contradiction: <b>user-friendly vs abuser-unfriendly</b></li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Limitations of technological approaches to security	<ul><li><b>Self-defeating</b>: clunky systems exceed the <b>compliance budget</b></li><li><b>Make things worse</b>: false burglar alarms</li><li><b>'Bolt-on, drop-off'</b> single fixed defence</li><li><b>Solution/supply-driven</b>: canalises responses (CCTV rush, white elephants)</li><li><b>Simplistic techno-determinism</b>: ignores complex adaptive systems</li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Why are these not inherent limitations of security technology?	They apply only to tech that is <b>poorly designed</b>, <b>over-relied on in isolation</b>, <b>incorporated too late</b>, <b>rigid</b>, or <b>not adaptable/upgradable</b> during its lifetime.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Wheelie bin: what failure does it illustrate and what was the design response?	<b>Failure to 'think thief'</b>: 'a receptacle for grime or a tool for crime?' Burglars use it as a <b>leg-up</b>. A trapping lid was rejected (child safety). Response: the <b>No ClimBIN</b> (Jenny Loqvist, Griffith University, 2008).	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Example of 'failure to think drug user'	Phone boxes with sides obscured by adverts became convenient places for drug users to inject, one even under an advert for a drug-help organisation.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	What is a crime harvest?	<b>Ken Pease</b>: a new product, not designed against crime, generates a wave of crime (e.g. 'What made Queen Victoria go red?').	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	What are 'paranoid products'?	A warning against <b>obsession</b> with security: over-secured designs that sacrifice usability.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Define Design Against Crime (DAC)	Uses the <b>tools, processes and products of design</b>, in partnership with agencies, companies, individuals and communities, to <b>prevent all kinds of crime</b> (incl. ASB, drugs, terrorism) and <b>promote quality of life/sustainable living</b>, via designs that are <b>fit for purpose</b> and contextually appropriate.	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Scope of Design Against Crime, with examples	<ul><li>Secure products (Puma bike; milled vs hammered coins against clipping)</li><li>Security products (clothes tagging device)</li><li>Security components (brand protection)</li><li>Security systems (chips protecting alpacas)</li><li>Security communication/art</li><li>Secure clothing (jacket camera, anti-theft bag)</li><li>Secure places (Maiden Castle, Sandy Hook School)</li><li>Secure communities</li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Is design a product or a process? What is reframing?	<b>Both.</b> SCP/CPTED practitioners see end products, but design is fundamentally a <b>process and way of thinking</b>, covering <b>problem-finding/formulation</b> as well as solving. <b>Reframing</b> = redefining the problem (e.g. the CT litter bin, UT Sydney).	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	The four elements of the Security Function Framework	<ul><li><b>Purpose</b>: what crime problem does it address?</li><li><b>Niche</b>: how does it fit the security ecosystem?</li><li><b>Mechanism</b>: how does it prevent crime?</li><li><b>Technology</b>: how is it realised (materials, construction, operation)?</li></ul>	SecTech L5 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L5 Crime Prevention & Design Against Crime	Stop Thief Chair: mechanism and technicality	<b>Mechanism:</b> physical <b>anchorage</b> of the bag (easier for the user to release); <b>mobilises people</b> as preventers (surveillance, reaction); bag within <b>personal defensible space</b>; <b>deters</b> by raising perceived risk.<br><b>Technicality:</b> <b>twin notch</b> in the leading edge of the seat; the bag handle is held between the seat and the back of the user's knees.	SecTech L5 concept
