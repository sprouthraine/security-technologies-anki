Basic	Security Technologies::L6 Law Enforcement (Radar, Taser, Breathalyser)	Describe the basic principle of radar ranging	<ol><li>A radio wave is transmitted from the antenna at t = 0</li><li>It reaches the object (t_travel) and <b>scatters in all directions</b></li><li>Part returns to the antenna (t_return); t_total = t_travel + t_return</li><li><b>R = c·t_total / 2</b></li></ol>	SecTech L6 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L6 Law Enforcement (Radar, Taser, Breathalyser)	What is range resolution and what does it depend on?	The <b>precision</b> of the range measurement (the uncertainty in object position). It depends <b>only on bandwidth B</b>: <b>ΔR = c/2B</b>. E.g. R = 100 m, ΔR = 2 m, so the object is at 99–101 m.	SecTech L6 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L6 Law Enforcement (Radar, Taser, Breathalyser)	Define bandwidth	The <b>range of frequencies</b> within a wave or signal (real signals are made of many frequencies, unlike a pure sinusoid).	SecTech L6 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L6 Law Enforcement (Radar, Taser, Breathalyser)	What is relative permittivity and why does it matter for GPR?	εᵣ = a material's ability to <b>store electrical energy</b>. When the radar wave meets a material of <b>different εᵣ</b>, a portion is <b>reflected</b> back to the antenna, forming the image.	SecTech L6 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L6 Law Enforcement (Radar, Taser, Breathalyser)	The image-quality vs depth trade-off in radar	<ul><li><b>High frequency:</b> can hold more <b>bandwidth</b>, so <b>better range resolution / image quality</b>, but more attenuation</li><li><b>Low frequency:</b> <b>less attenuation</b>, so <b>deeper penetration</b>, but poorer resolution</li></ul>Modern GPRs are <b>tunable</b>.	SecTech L6 concept

Basic	Security Technologies::L6 Law Enforcement (Radar, Taser, Breathalyser)	Explain the attenuation problem in through-the-wall radar	Magnified at the <b>air–wall interface</b> (significant scattering). At <b>high frequencies</b> only a tiny proportion penetrates brick/concrete, and the weak reflection must come <b>back through the wall</b>, so the signal is too weak for the radar to operate.	SecTech L6 concept

Basic	Security Technologies::L6 Law Enforcement (Radar, Taser, Breathalyser)	Physiological effect of a Taser	Skeletal muscle (~40% of body mass) is controlled by electrical impulses via <b>motor nerves</b>. Taser pulses <b>jolt the motor nerves</b>, causing <b>involuntary contraction</b>, so the target spasms, drops and loses motor control.	SecTech L6 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L6 Law Enforcement (Radar, Taser, Breathalyser)	State Ohm's law in words	The current through a conductor is <b>proportional to the voltage</b>; the constant of proportionality relates to the <b>resistance R</b>, which is inversely proportional to the current: <b>I = V/R</b>, V = IR. Muscle contraction rates <b>&gt; 70 per second</b> are dangerously strong (the disease tetanus causes contraction at this rate). The Taser PRF is <b>19</b>, far below, enough to disable without long-term damage.	SecTech L6 concept

Compare with a mains-voltage shock.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li><b>I = Q/t</b>: 100 µC × 19 / 1 s = <b>1.9 mA</b></li><li>About <b>1%</b> of the current causing <b>ventricular fibrillation</b></li><li>PRF 19 is far below the <b>tetanus region (&gt; 70/s)</b></li><li>Mains: I = V/R; 240 V / 100 kΩ = 2.4 mA (tingle, safe); 240 V / 10 kΩ = 24 mA (probably lethal)</li><li>Hence it is <b>current, not voltage, that kills</b>; the Taser is high voltage but low current</li></ul>	SecTech L6 exam-style
