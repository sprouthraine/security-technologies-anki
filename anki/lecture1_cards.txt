Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	What is a wave? (definition + key idea)	<b>Definition:</b> a time-varying quantity that is also a function of position.<br><ul><li>Waves <b>transport energy without transporting matter</b></li><li>Analogy: a Mexican wave in a stadium (energy moves round, people stay put)</li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	What two properties must a medium have for a mechanical wave to travel through it? Define each.	<ul><li><b>Elasticity:</b> ability to return to original size/shape after being deformed (stretched/compressed)</li><li><b>Inertia:</b> resistance of an object to a change in its velocity</li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	How is a mechanical wave produced? Distinguish a wave pulse from a periodic wave.	An initial input of energy (a disturbance) into the medium, e.g. a stone thrown into a pond or a flick of a string.<br><ul><li><b>Single</b> disturbance gives a <b>wave pulse</b></li><li><b>Continuous</b> regular disturbance gives a <b>periodic (sinusoidal) wave</b></li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	What is the difference between wave speed and particle velocity?	<ul><li><b>Wave speed (v):</b> speed at which the <b>energy</b> propagates through the medium</li><li><b>Particle velocity:</b> speed at which the <b>particles</b> of the medium vibrate about equilibrium</li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Longitudinal wave: definition and example	Vibrations are <b>parallel</b> to the direction of propagation. They create <b>compressions</b> and <b>rarefactions</b>.<br>Example: <b>sound</b>, a pressure wave from vibrating sources (loudspeaker, larynx).	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Transverse wave: definition and examples	Vibrations are <b>at right angles</b> to the direction of energy travel (particles move up and down about equilibrium).<br>Examples: waves on a string, water waves, <b>all EM waves</b>.	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Mechanical vs electromagnetic waves: medium, type, examples	<ul><li><b>Mechanical:</b> need a medium; longitudinal <b>or</b> transverse; e.g. sound, water waves, guitar string, seismic waves</li><li><b>EM:</b> no medium (travel in a vacuum at c); <b>transverse only</b>; perpendicular E and B field oscillations; e.g. light, microwaves, X-rays, radio</li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	What sound does a massive explosion make in space? Why?	<b>None.</b> Sound is a mechanical (longitudinal pressure) wave and needs a medium. Space is a vacuum.	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Define amplitude (and what it represents)	The maximum disturbance from the undisturbed (equilibrium) position. It represents the <b>power</b> of the wave (loud sound = high amplitude). Units depend on the quantity measured (dB, V, lumens).	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Define wavelength (λ)	The distance over which the wave shape repeats: the distance between the same points on two adjacent waves (e.g. crest to crest). Unit: <b>m</b>.	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Define frequency and time period	<ul><li><b>Frequency (f):</b> number of wave cycles per second (Hz). For sound it relates to pitch.</li><li><b>Period (T = 1/f):</b> time for the wave pattern to advance one wavelength (s)</li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Why must we use dimensional analysis?	It identifies the <b>base SI units</b> of physical quantities. This allows real-world interpretation and comparison between measures (e.g. N = m·kg·s⁻²).	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	How are gamma rays and X-rays of the same wavelength (e.g. 0.1 nm) distinguished?	By their <b>source</b>: gamma rays come from <b>nuclear radioactivity</b> (the nucleus); X-rays originate from <b>electrons</b>.	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Give a property/application of each EM band: gamma, X-ray, infrared, microwave, radio	<ul><li><b>Gamma:</b> absorbed by cells, used to kill bacteria in food</li><li><b>X-ray:</b> penetrates skin/tissue but not bone (medical imaging, baggage scanners)</li><li><b>Infrared:</b> body heat (thermal imaging, night vision)</li><li><b>Microwave:</b> similar frequency to water molecules (ovens); mm-wave scanners, radar</li><li><b>Radio:</b> communications (cellular, WiFi, satellite), radar, ATC, GPS, RFID</li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	What are ultrasound and infrasound? Give uses of each.	<ul><li><b>Ultrasound (&gt; 20 kHz):</b> medical imaging, non-destructive testing (pipeline fractures), bat sonar</li><li><b>Infrasound (&lt; 20 Hz):</b> communication by elephants, giraffes and whales; detecting volcanic eruptions</li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Interference: what is it, the two types, and the condition required?	Superposition of two waves giving a resultant with higher or lower amplitude.<br><ul><li><b>Constructive:</b> waves in phase reinforce, giving larger amplitude</li><li><b>Destructive:</b> waves in antiphase cancel</li><li><b>Condition:</b> same or similar frequency (a radio wave and an X-ray won't interfere)</li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	What is the Doppler effect?	A change in frequency/wavelength (a <b>Doppler shift</b>) when source and receiver <b>move relative to each other</b>.<br><ul><li>Approaching: frequency increases (higher pitch)</li><li>Receding: frequency decreases (lower pitch)</li></ul>Application: <b>radar speed guns</b>.	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Fourier Series vs Fourier Transform	<ul><li><b>Fourier Series:</b> any <b>periodic</b> function expressed as an infinite sum of sines and cosines</li><li><b>Fourier Transform:</b> generalisation to <b>non-periodic</b> functions; decomposes a <b>time-domain</b> signal into its <b>frequency-domain</b> components (spectral decomposition)</li></ul>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	What does the magnitude squared of the Fourier Transform, |F(ω)|², represent?	The amount of <b>power</b> a signal has at a particular frequency.	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Why are Fourier methods relevant to real-world security signals?	Real signals (speech, WiFi, radar returns) are not single-frequency sinusoids but contain <b>many frequencies</b>. The FT reveals their spectral content, e.g. checking a WiFi signal against its <b>spectral mask</b>.	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	What did James Clerk Maxwell show?	That electricity and magnetism are manifestations of the <b>same phenomenon</b>, travelling together perpendicularly as a transverse wave at the speed of light (<b>Maxwell's equations</b>, the 'second great unification of physics').	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	State the right-hand grip rule	Wrap the right hand around a current-carrying wire with the <b>thumb pointing in the direction of current</b>. The <b>curled fingers</b> show the direction of the magnetic field.	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Three ways to increase the strength of an electromagnet	<ol><li>Place an <b>iron core</b> in the coil</li><li>Add <b>more turns</b></li><li>Increase the <b>current</b></li></ol>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	State Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction (words)	The induced EMF (voltage) in a closed circuit is equal to the <b>rate of change of magnetic flux</b> through it. Moving a magnet through a coil induces an EMF, and a current if the circuit is complete.	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Four ways to maximise the EMF induced in a coil	<ol><li>Move the magnet <b>faster</b></li><li>Use a <b>stronger</b> magnet</li><li><b>More turns</b> in the coil</li><li><b>Larger coil area</b></li></ol>	SecTech L1 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	Applications of Faraday's law relevant to security technology	Electric motors, generators, transformers, <b>RFID tags</b> (passive tags powered by induction), <b>pulse-induction metal detectors</b>.	SecTech L1 concept

Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	🧮 CALCULATION<br>A 440 Hz note travels in air at 20 °C (343 m/s). Find its wavelength (2 d.p.).	λ = v/f = 343/440 = <b>0.78 m</b>	SecTech L1 calculation
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	🧮 CALCULATION<br>Find the wavelength of (a) a 2.4 GHz WiFi signal and (b) a 25 GHz mm-wave scanner.	<ul><li>(a) λ = c/f = 3×10⁸ / 2.4×10⁹ = <b>0.125 m</b></li><li>(b) λ = 3×10⁸ / 25×10⁹ = <b>0.012 m = 12 mm</b> (hence 'millimetre waves')</li></ul>	SecTech L1 calculation
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	🧮 CALCULATION<br>A string has tension 100 N and linear mass density 0.01 kg/m. Find the wave speed.	v = √(F/µ) = √(100/0.01) = √10000 = <b>100 m/s</b>	SecTech L1 calculation
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	🧮 CALCULATION<br>A 1000 Hz siren approaches a pedestrian at 20 m/s (sound speed 343 m/s). Find Δf and the heard frequency.	Δf = (Δv/c)·f₀ = (20/343) × 1000 = <b>58.3 Hz</b><br>Heard ≈ <b>1058.3 Hz</b> (higher pitch); about 941.7 Hz when receding.	SecTech L1 calculation
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	🧮 CALCULATION<br>A radar speed gun transmits at 24 GHz; a car approaches at 30 m/s. Find the Doppler shift (lecture formula).	Δf = (30 / 3×10⁸) × 24×10⁹ = <b>2400 Hz</b><br>(A real two-way radar shift would be 2vf₀/c = 4.8 kHz.)	SecTech L1 calculation
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	🧮 CALCULATION<br>The magnetic flux through a coil falls from 0.5 Wb to 0.1 Wb in 0.2 s. Find |EMF|.	|ε| = ΔΦ/Δt = (0.5 − 0.1)/0.2 = <b>2 V</b>	SecTech L1 calculation

Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[6 marks] Compare mechanical and electromagnetic waves. In your answer explain the difference between longitudinal and transverse waves and give examples of each.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li>Mechanical waves need a <b>medium</b> with elasticity and inertia; EM waves need <b>no medium</b> (travel in a vacuum)</li><li>EM waves travel at <b>c ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s</b> in a vacuum; mechanical wave speed depends on the medium (e.g. sound 343 m/s in air, 5960 m/s in steel)</li><li>EM waves = oscillating <b>electric and magnetic fields at right angles</b></li><li><b>Longitudinal:</b> vibrations parallel to propagation; compressions and rarefactions; e.g. <b>sound</b></li><li><b>Transverse:</b> vibrations perpendicular to propagation; e.g. string, water, <b>all EM waves</b></li><li>Mechanical can be either type; EM are <b>only transverse</b></li></ul>	SecTech L1 exam-style
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[5 marks] Sketch/describe the electromagnetic spectrum. Order the bands, indicate how frequency and wavelength vary, and give a security technology that operates in each band.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li>Order (high f / short λ to low f / long λ): <b>gamma, X-ray, UV, visible, IR, microwave, radio</b></li><li>f and λ are inversely related: <b>c = fλ</b></li><li>Visible light is the reference point: <b>400–700 nm</b></li><li>Examples: gamma (radiological detection), X-ray (baggage/CT/backscatter scanners), IR (thermal imaging/night vision), microwave/mm-wave (body scanners, radar), radio (RFID, GPR, through-wall radar, communications)</li><li>Bands can overlap (gamma/X-ray distinguished by source)</li></ul>	SecTech L1 exam-style
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[4 marks] Explain the Doppler effect using the example of a police car siren, state the equation for the Doppler shift, and describe one security application.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li>Change in frequency when source and receiver move <b>relative to each other</b></li><li>Car approaching: waves compressed, <b>higher frequency/pitch</b>; receding: <b>lower</b></li><li><b>Δf = (Δv/c)·f₀</b> (Δv = relative velocity, f₀ = emitted frequency); positive approaching, negative receding</li><li>Application: <b>radar speed gun</b> measures vehicle speed from Δf; also through-wall radar detecting moving targets / micro-Doppler</li></ul>	SecTech L1 exam-style
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[5 marks] State Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction, give its equation, and explain how it is exploited in a security technology of your choice.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li>Induced EMF in a closed circuit = <b>rate of change of magnetic flux</b>; <b>ε = −ΔΦ/Δt</b>, Φ in webers</li><li>A magnet moving through a coil induces a voltage; current flows if the circuit is closed</li><li>EMF increased by faster motion, stronger magnet, more turns, bigger area</li><li><b>Example (RFID):</b> the reader's changing EM field induces a current in the tag coil, charging a capacitor that powers the IC (no battery)</li><li><b>OR (metal detector):</b> a pulse in the coil creates a field; its collapse induces a reflected pulse; metal prolongs it beyond ~30 µs</li></ul>	SecTech L1 exam-style
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[4 marks] Explain why real-world signals cannot be described by a single sine wave, and how the Fourier Series and Fourier Transform are used to analyse them.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li>Real signals (speech, WiFi) contain <b>many frequencies</b></li><li><b>Fourier Series:</b> any <b>periodic</b> signal = weighted sum of sines and cosines (e.g. square wave = odd harmonics; more terms give a better approximation)</li><li><b>Fourier Transform:</b> for <b>non-periodic</b> signals; converts the <b>time domain to the frequency domain</b> (inverse FT goes back)</li><li>|F(ω)|² = power at each frequency; e.g. a WiFi signal must conform to its <b>spectral mask</b></li></ul>	SecTech L1 exam-style
Basic	Security Technologies::L1 Waves & Fields	📝 EXAM-STYLE<br>[4 marks] Define amplitude, wavelength, frequency and time period, and derive the wave equation v = fλ.	<b>Marking points:</b><br><ul><li>Amplitude: max displacement from equilibrium (power)</li><li>Wavelength: distance between adjacent identical points (m)</li><li>Frequency: cycles per second (Hz); period T: time to advance one wavelength, <b>T = 1/f</b></li><li>Speed = distance/time = λ/T, so <b>v = fλ</b> (for EM waves <b>c = fλ</b>)</li></ul>	SecTech L1 exam-style
