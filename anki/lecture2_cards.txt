Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Describe the Bohr model of the atom. Why is it called a 'planetary' model?	<ul><li>Negatively charged <b>electrons orbit</b> a central <b>nucleus</b> of positive <b>protons</b> and neutral <b>neutrons</b> (nucleons)</li><li>Equal numbers of protons and electrons, so <b>net charge zero</b></li><li>Electrons sit in fixed-energy <b>shells</b></li><li>'Planetary': electrons are planets orbiting the sun (nucleus); the <b>electrostatic force</b> plays the role of gravity</li></ul>	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	What are nucleons? What is the atomic number Z?	<b>Nucleons</b> = protons + neutrons (particles in the nucleus). <b>Z</b> = number of protons; the periodic table is ordered by increasing Z.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	What is the ground state of an atom?	When electrons occupy the <b>lowest energy levels</b>, filling the shells from n = 1, then n = 2, n = 3, and so on.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	What are valence electrons and why do they matter?	The <b>outermost</b> (highest-energy shell) electrons. They determine how an atom <b>bonds</b> with other atoms, i.e. its <b>reactivity</b>.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Define an isotope. Give the carbon example.	Same <b>atomic number Z</b> (same element), <b>different number of neutrons</b> N (so different A). C-12 (6n), C-13 (7n), C-14 (8n, unstable). Over 100 elements, but over 2000 isotopes.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Cation vs anion	<ul><li><b>Cation:</b> positive ion, has <b>lost electrons</b> (not gained protons)</li><li><b>Anion:</b> negative ion, has <b>gained electrons</b></li></ul>	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Properties of Group 1 alkali metals	1 valence electron, so <b>highly reactive</b> (more so down the group); form +1 cations; soft (cut with a knife); shiny surface tarnishes by oxidation, so they are <b>stored in oil</b>.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Properties of transition metals (groups 3–12)	Good conductors of heat and electricity, malleable, less reactive than alkali metals, high density and melting point (except mercury). E.g. Fe, Cu, Ag, Au.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	What are metalloids and why are they useful?	Elements on the step-like boundary between metals and non-metals, with properties of both. Si and Ge are <b>semiconductors</b>, used in electronic components.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Properties and a use of the noble gases (group 18)	<b>Full outer shells</b>, so highly stable/unreactive; odourless, colourless, monatomic. Used where reactions such as oxidation are unwanted, e.g. <b>argon in light bulbs</b> protects the tungsten filament.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	What is mass spectrometry and what does it measure?	An analytical technique to <b>identify substances, even in trace amounts</b>, quickly and accurately. It measures the masses and abundances of atoms/molecules by their <b>mass-to-charge ratio (m/z)</b>.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Three ionisation methods used in mass spectrometry	<ul><li><b>Electron ionisation:</b> electron beam knocks off electrons</li><li><b>Chemical ionisation:</b> ions created through chemical reactions</li><li><b>Electrospray ionisation:</b> high voltage applied to an aerosol sprayed through a nozzle</li></ul>	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	ToF vs magnetic sector mass spectrometer: how are ions separated?	<ul><li><b>Time-of-Flight:</b> an <b>electric field</b> accelerates ions; <b>lighter ions travel faster</b> and the flight time to the detector is measured</li><li><b>Magnetic sector:</b> a <b>magnetic field</b> bends ions along curved paths depending on charge-to-mass ratio; they hit different detector regions</li></ul>	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	How do you read a mass spectrograph?	Each peak = a detected ion. <b>Peak height = abundance</b>; <b>x-axis position = mass-to-charge ratio</b>. Each substance has a <b>unique signature</b> (e.g. cocaine, TNT).	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Security applications of mass spectrometry	Drug detection, explosive detection, blood-alcohol measurement, identifying radioactive materials, forensic trace evidence.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Define radioactivity / radioactive decay	The <b>spontaneous disintegration of the nuclei of some nuclides</b> (radionuclides): unstable nuclei emit α, β⁻ or β⁺ particles, sometimes with γ rays, to become stable. α/β emission changes Z, creating a <b>new element</b>.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Alpha particle: composition, charge, mass, effect on the nucleus	<b>2 protons + 2 neutrons</b> (helium nucleus ⁴₂He). Charge <b>+2e</b>, mass <b>4 mᵤ</b>, ~3×10⁷ m/s. <b>Z −2, A −4</b>.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Beta-minus particle: what is it, where does it come from, effect on the nucleus?	An <b>electron emitted from the nucleus</b>, NOT from the electron shells. A <b>neutron changes into a proton</b>. Charge −e, mass 1/1836 mᵤ. <b>Z +1, N −1, A unchanged</b>.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Beta-plus (positron): what is it and its effect on the nucleus?	A positively charged electron (anti-electron). A <b>proton changes into a neutron</b>. Charge +e, mass 1/1836 mᵤ. <b>Z −1, N +1, A unchanged</b>.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Gamma rays: nature and effect on the nucleus	<b>EM waves</b> emitted from the nucleus after an energy change. <b>No charge, no mass</b>, no change to protons or neutrons. Highest frequency / shortest λ (10⁻¹⁰ to 2×10⁻¹³ m).	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Penetration of α, β, γ: range in air and what stops each	<ul><li><b>α:</b> a few cm in air; stopped by <b>paper or skin</b> (very dangerous if ingested)</li><li><b>β:</b> a few m in air; stopped by <b>wood/plastic/thin metal</b></li><li><b>γ:</b> large distances; only stopped by <b>high-Z materials, e.g. lead</b></li></ul>	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	What is ionising radiation? Rank α, β, γ by ionising power.	Radiation able to <b>eject electrons</b> from atoms/molecules, leaving ions. <b>α &gt; β &gt; γ</b>. Effects: tissue damage, radiation sickness, cancer, mutation, death. X-rays are non-nuclear ionising radiation.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Why doesn't the nucleus fly apart from proton repulsion?	The <b>strong force</b>: a short-range attractive force between nucleons. Its associated <b>binding energy</b> determines whether a nucleus is stable.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	State the two rules of nuclear stability and explain the second	<ul><li>(i) <b>N ≈ Z for Z &lt; 20</b></li><li>(ii) <b>N &gt; Z for Z &gt; 20</b>: extra neutrons separate the protons, reducing repulsion</li></ul>Smallest stable nucleus ¹₁H; largest <b>²⁰⁹₈₃Bi</b>.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	How do neutron-rich and proton-rich nuclides decay?	<ul><li><b>Neutron-rich</b> (above the stability line): <b>β⁻</b> (n → p)</li><li><b>Proton-rich</b> (below the line): <b>β⁺</b> (lighter) or <b>α</b> (heavier) (p → n)</li></ul>	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Define activity and the becquerel	Activity = rate at which a radioactive material emits radiation. <b>1 Bq = 1 disintegration per second</b> (SI: s⁻¹). 3.7×10¹⁰ Bq = 1 curie.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Define radioactive half-life	The time in which the amount of a radionuclide <b>decays to half its original value</b>. All radioactive decay follows a characteristic (exponential) decay curve.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	What is a decay chain (radioactive series)?	An unstable parent decays to an <b>unstable daughter</b>, which decays again, and so on, until a <b>stable</b> nuclide is reached. Shown on an A vs Z plot, e.g. Th-228 → Pb-208.	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Why is Polonium-210 an effective assassination poison?	<ul><li><b>Alpha emitter</b>: contained by glass, so easily smuggled through airports</li><li>Once <b>ingested</b>, alpha is the most strongly ionising radiation, causing DNA damage, cell destruction and organ failure</li><li>Max safe dose only <b>7 pg</b>; a speck is fatal</li><li>Biological half-life ~30 days; physical half-life 138 days (intensely radioactive)</li></ul>	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	Evidence that the Litvinenko poisoning was state-sponsored	<ul><li>Po-210 extremely rare: ~<b>100 g/year</b>, made in <b>nuclear reactors</b></li><li>Amount used would cost <b>tens of millions of dollars</b></li><li>Suspects (Lugovoy, Kovtun) protected from extradition by Russia</li><li>Inquiry (2016): 'probably' FSB, 'probably' approved by Putin</li></ul>	SecTech L2 concept
Basic	Security Technologies::L2 Structure of Matter & Nuclear Science	How was the Litvinenko radiation detected and identified?	<ul><li><b>Geiger counter:</b> ionising events in a Geiger–Müller tube, read out as counts per second / clicks</li><li><b>Alpha-particle spectrometer:</b> identifies the isotope by characteristic energy (Po-210 alphas = <b>5.3 MeV</b>)</li><li>A trail of contamination across London ('Hansel and Gretel breadcrumbs'): hotel tea room, teapot</li></ul>	SecTech L2 concept
